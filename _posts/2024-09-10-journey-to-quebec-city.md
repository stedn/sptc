We haven't flown anywhere in five years, which sounds like a sacrifice until you realize how much of what people actually want out of flying somewhere far away is really just the feeling of otherness, of being somewhere that doesn't sound or smell or taste like home. We still wanted that feeling. We just wanted to get to it without a boarding pass. Quebec City turned out to be exactly that: our exotic French getaway, without the flight. The only rule we gave ourselves was that we had to get there entirely by train, Madison to Detroit to Montreal to Quebec, one continuous rail line strung together out of a handful of connections. It turned into a much bigger adventure than we expected, daring border crossing included, and by the time we got home we'd forgotten to miss the plane at all.

## Detroit: the gods laughing at us

Sometimes we wonder if the gods are actually laughing at us. We got into Detroit the day before the Grand Prix, which we did not plan for and did not see coming until we were already there. The city had entirely blocked off a huge circle of downtown streets to build the race track overnight, and the concrete barriers didn't care that we had a train to catch. They cut off access to both the tunnel to Canada and the train station we needed, and for a while it genuinely looked like our whole itinerary was about to fall apart on day one.

We wandered the perimeter of that barricade for what felt like a very long time, retracing our steps, trying side streets that dead ended into more concrete, starting to do the math on what missing our connection would actually cost us. Eventually we found a hotel with a skywalk that happened to cross over the blocked off tracks, and we ducked inside, half expecting to get stopped by security, and followed it across. It let out, of all places, directly into a car museum. We walked past a room full of gleaming vintage automobiles to make our train, which felt like such an absurd, on the nose bit of irony that we couldn't even be mad about it anymore. We just started laughing.

## Montreal: a taste of Europe

Montreal, c'est incroyable! We came for the Tour de l'Île bike tour and stayed with the loveliest host family for our three day visit, the kind of hosts who don't just hand you a key and disappear. They rode around with us themselves, showing us their city instead of just describing it, including one late night ride through downtown Montreal with the streetlights reflecting off the wet pavement that we still think about. We don't often do city-only trips without some kind of outdoor component attached, but Montreal delivered everything we actually wanted out of a taste of Europe anyway: real transit that runs when it says it will, cycling infrastructure that treats bikes like actual transportation instead of an afterthought, and hosts who treated hanging out with two strangers on bikes as a fun way to spend an evening rather than an obligation.

## Quebec City: better than flying to France

Quebec City delivered on the whole reason we came in the first place. The food was fantastic in that specific way that makes you eat more than you planned to at every single meal, and the old town streets are exactly the kind of charming, cobblestoned, patisserie on every corner French streets we were hoping for when we first started plotting this trip. We genuinely couldn't tell you what we'd have been missing by actually flying all the way to France instead. Every argument our brains tried to make for why the real thing would have been better kept running into the same wall: we were standing right there, eating a croissant, listening to French on the street, and none of it required a single gallon of jet fuel.

On top of that, we managed to reach real outdoor wilderness without a car too, which is the part of these trips we're always secretly the most excited about. We hopped a shuttle from downtown for a 45 minute ride out to Jacques Cartier National Park, 258 square miles of it, river valleys and boreal forest stretching out further than we could see, and did a beautiful 9 mile hike once we got there. It's easy to assume that reaching a place like that requires a rental car and a parking lot, and it was satisfying every time to prove that assumption wrong.

## Back to Michigan Central

One more thing about Detroit before we let it go. As funny as the car museum detour was in the moment, it would have been so much more fitting if we could have caught our connection out of the old Michigan Central Station instead. It's a genuinely stunning building, recently restored after decades of sitting abandoned as one of the most photographed ruins in the country, but it's not running trains anymore. It sits there instead as a monument to exactly the kind of decline we keep writing about on this blog.

{% include ig-embed.html url="https://www.instagram.com/reel/C92TxA6Oz8E/" %}

Fun fact we couldn't stop thinking about the whole rest of the trip: the CEO of GM went on to become the U.S. Secretary of Defense and helped build the interstates with our tax dollars. That's a big part of why driving everywhere became the default in this country instead of an option among several. Trains would probably be cheaper and nicer today if all that public money had gone somewhere else, or better yet, gone toward the tracks instead of the roads.

There's at least some talk of eventually connecting Windsor and Detroit by passenger rail again, so maybe someday we'll get to ride through that beautiful old station instead of ducking through a gift shop full of hood ornaments to make our train.
