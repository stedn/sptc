This one's more of a maker story than a travel story. The habit started back on our cross-country bike trip in 2021 and 2022, when we built our first solar charging tandem ebike from scratch out of parts and stubbornness, mostly because nothing off the shelf did what we needed it to do. Ever since then the pattern's stayed pretty much the same: buy an ebike kit, bolt it onto whatever frame we've got lying around or find on the street, and see what fun thing comes out of it. The three builds below are separate projects, spaced out over a couple of years, not one continuous build, but they're all the same habit, and none of them would exist if we'd just been willing to buy something normal instead.

## Project one: the boat trailer

We'd picked up a sailboat that came with a car trailer, which isn't much use to two people who don't own a car and have no intention of getting one. So we set out to convert it to work behind a bike instead. We already had an off-the-shelf tag-along kids' bike trailer sitting around, one that had turned out to be too wobbly to actually pull our nieces and nephews around safely, and it turned out to be almost the perfect base for towing a boat instead of a toddler. The original boat trailer hitch had a huge, heavy steel bar bolted on that was completely unnecessary for the size of our boat, so we had some fun with an angle grinder cutting it down to just what we actually needed, and adapting the connection so it wouldn't chew up the rear rack the way the first attempt did.

That setup earned us a spot in what we're calling the unofficial Carry Shit Olympics: sailing plus cycling plus towing, all at once, exactly as ridiculous as it sounds, and exactly the kind of ridiculous we've come to enjoy. There's a specific kind of pride that comes from towing a literal sailboat behind a bicycle down a Madison bike path and getting looks from drivers stuck in traffic next to you.

## Project two: the etrike

A separate build from the boat trailer, though it shares the same DNA: once the etrike itself came together, the first real test was a shakedown ride out to Little Society Farm near Devils Lake, one of the better CSA and WWOOF setups in Wisconsin, and it went off almost without a hitch. We were grateful for sunshine and for the panels pushing us along the whole way, and even more grateful to friends who let us come visit and see the farm in person. We came away from that first ride genuinely impressed with how the whole machine held up, with just a few more modifications planned before we'd trust it with something longer.

## 220 miles to the Energy Fair

The real proof of concept came later that summer: a 220 mile solo ride to the Midwest Renewable Energy Association's Energy Fair, the longest single-day solar ebike ride we'd attempted by a wide margin. We rebuilt the etrike beforehand, tightening up everything the shakedown ride had exposed, and it rode better than it ever had. Which was good, because there was also a talk to give once we got there, on solar ebikes of all things, delivered by someone who'd just spent an entire day proving the concept to himself the hard way.

## Project three: the velomobile

A whole separate build again, further down the timeline: a proper velomobile, an electric-assist enclosed trike built around a fuselage we found sitting on the street at the UW engineering campus, of all places, apparently discarded by some student project we never got the full story on. It's finished now, at least in its current form, and riding it feels like piloting a small aircraft that happens to have pedals. The goal is still to cover the whole fuselage in solar panels so the thing can charge itself as it goes, turning it into a fully self-sufficient little pod that never needs to plug into anything.

{% include ig-embed.html url="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DDBUkpaOaGM/" %}

We'll post an update once the panels are actually on. For now it's already the strangest, most fun thing we've ever pedaled, and we mean that as the highest possible compliment.
